# OpenSTEM Research Match — Evidence-first launch playbook

The limiting factor is not site design. It is **two-sided trust and liquidity**: without relevant, reviewed research calls students have no reason to return; without demonstrably suitable students professors have no reason to post.

## Decision: start with 2 academic clusters, not every discipline simultaneously

**Suggested initial focus:** Physics/Quantum Computing and Computer Science/Scientific ML. These are hypotheses, not proven high-conversion niches; change them if actual researcher demand differs. Show other disciplines on the platform but do not promise coverage.

## Weeks 1–2: build supply that can be evaluated

- Recruit **15–25 opt-in student applications** with portfolios (course projects count if accurately labeled). Invite students from existing OpenSTEM Lab, Library and Replicate usage, and the existing Discord. This is a target, not a claim about existing members.
- Ask each student for ONE research question + method + evidence link + weekly availability + time zone. Review evidence rather than GPA or institution prestige.
- Manually approve only accurate profiles; offer a short checklist for incomplete ones. Measure how many applications are usable and how long they take to review.

## Weeks 2–4: recruit researcher demand before scaling students

- Identify **10–20 relevant labs or supervisors** through university departmental websites and genuine public research projects. Reach out individually with a real fit hypothesis, not a bulk promise of “top students.”
- Ask for **2–5 genuine, scoped project briefs** with supervision, time requirement and compensation/funding. Verify affiliation with independent institutional evidence before toggling “identity checked.”
- Do not publish a paid role without knowing basic compensation terms; do not publish unpaid work without appropriate legality/institutional and ethical review.
- If you cannot get real briefs, treat student acquisition as prelaunch research readiness rather than implying opportunities exist.

## Weeks 4–8: run the actual matching loop

1. Students filter real calls; they submit PRIVATE project-specific interest with an evidence link.
2. Admin inspects applicant evidence, eligibility, time zone and availability and confirms whether the researcher is still accepting interest.
3. Admin asks each side to consent to a specific introduction before sending contact details; there is no automatic messaging.
4. The pair agrees on a charter and 30-day milestones. Do not create hidden unpaid labor expectations.
5. Request permission before publishing anonymized or named outcomes; never fabricate placements, partnerships or testimonials.

## A measurement dashboard to maintain externally

| KPI | How to calculate | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified student rate | Evidence-reviewed eligible students / all student applications | Measures student-side supply quality |
| Verified public brief rate | Approved identity-checked public calls / all researcher briefs | Measures professor-side supply |
| Relevant-interest rate | Project interests with demonstrable skills / total project interests | Measures genuine match quality |
| Intro consent rate | Introductions with approval from both sides / proposed introductions | Measures trust and participation |
| Response turnaround | Median time from submission to real human first response | Measures service reliability |
| 30-day activation | Introduced pairs agreeing to a charter and first deliverable / introductions | Measures whether matches become collaboration |
| 30-day continuation | Active productive partnerships at day 30 / pairs that began | Measures partnership quality |
| Source-by-role yield | Qualified students or verified briefs per acquisition source | Guides OpenSTEM cross-promotion |

**No numeric conversion rates are assumed**. The system does not have an analytics SDK. Record these measures privately; use only lawful and consensual information.

## Ethical growth loops designed into the site

- **Student loop:** library/lab/paper → one reproducible portfolio exercise → reviewed profile → share with a suitable classmate.
- **Professor loop:** clearly scoped project brief → credible relevant profiles or private interest → small consented introduction → successful first research deliverable → optional referral to a colleague.
- **Community loop:** Discord subject discussion → learning artifact → application → research opportunity. Do not auto-DM or scrape students/professors into the service.

## Templates for human outreach (personalize, avoid mass spam)

**Researcher:** "I came across your lab's work on [specific project]. We are piloting OpenSTEM Research Match to help researchers publish small, supervised research tasks and review relevant student work samples. Would a 6–8 week project with clearly specified methods and weekly supervision be useful for your group? I can send the brief format; there is no promise of placements."

**Student:** "If you're interested in [specific method/topic], could you share one notebook, report or poster showing what you've tried? OpenSTEM Research Match is testing an evidence-led student directory and will only share your details publicly with your permission. Research openings are not guaranteed."

## Stop/pivot signals

- **No verified researcher calls after 20 genuinely relevant conversations:** reconsider the niche, brief length, reviewer process and professor benefit before buying traffic.
- **Many student signups but little evidence:** simplify portfolio examples and supply a structured learning sprint rather than lowering review standards.
- **Mentors consistently miss check-ins or scope shifts:** address time/compensation/supervision terms, not just matching accuracy.
- **No measurable 30-day progress among consented pairs:** do not advertise a successful matching system; collect structured feedback and alter the charter/workflow.
